PROPOSED PRE-ANALYSIS PROTOCOL, CENTRAL R1 REVISION
The Order of Transformation, 17 October 2026
Status: design specification. Not registered, recruited, approved, or executed.
This protocol accompanies the complete discussion in the manuscript protocol and dictionary appendices.

QUESTION AND UNIT
Compare two implementation policies for data remediation and decision support in
repeated replenishment forecasting. Unit: operational team with independent manager,
pipeline and inventory responsibility. Merge shared-staff/shared-inventory units into
larger assignment clusters before randomization. Population inference targets equal
weight over eligible teams; a separately declared capacity-weighted contrast is secondary.
No firm or partner has committed units or granted access.

ELIGIBILITY
Lock the roster after four baseline weeks. Require complete decision/accounting logs,
a repeatable manual process, shadow-scoring ability, and frozen outcome definitions.
Do not exclude units after seeing treatment responses. Record baseline source quality,
team size, operating performance, staff and readiness. Preserve the full assignment roster.

COMPONENTS AND TIMING
F: frozen source-defect remediation list, validation tests, ownership, freshness checks.
A: one engine/interface version, workflow integration, training and recommendation logs.
Dates are synchronized across arms; exact dates must be frozen before allocation.
Weeks -4 to -1: baseline. Weeks 1-4: first component. Weeks 5-8: second component.
Weeks 9-16: common completed-system observation; endpoint is weeks 13-16.
F-first: F in phase 1, A in phase 2. Tools-first: A in phase 1, F in phase 2.
Maintain existing installed components. Both get the same completed-system validation
block at the end of week 8. The early tools demo is inside A's fixed budget; F-first
gets the same A onboarding allocation on its later arrival. Thus early exposure differs.
Neither final capital equality nor equal nominal budget means equal information histories.
To match the baseline model, F-first onboarding consists of orientation and unscored
practice, with only the common scored validation before follow-up. If onboarding
requires an extra scored q1 trial, the appropriate alternative model gives F-first
two scored q1 trials; tools-first can no longer dominate its willingness state.
Freeze this distinction before assignment and audit feedback actually observed.
No simultaneous arm is included. Week 25-28 follow-up is exploratory unless funded
and specified before randomization.

BUDGET AND TECHNICAL ACCEPTANCE
Fixed implementation-hour and purchased-service allocations for each component,
independent of order. Count actual labor, downtime, training, demonstrations and extra
remediation in a cost ledger. Decision rights, incentives and tool version are fixed.
Both arms must pass every prespecified critical-field acceptance test. Report residual
quality with uncertainty. Failure to reject a quality gap is not evidence of equivalence.
If final technical quality differs materially, interpret ITT as a rollout/fidelity policy
effect, not evidence of path dependence under matched final resources.

ASSIGNMENT
1:1 within locked blocks: site, source defects, size, baseline outcome. Prefer blocks
of four; document any incomplete block handling before drawing allocation. Generate
an auditable seed only after roster lock. Allocation is at team/merged-cluster level.

PRIMARY POTENTIAL OUTCOME AND ESTIMAND
s=(order, phase dates, assigned intensity, validation schedule).
Y_i,T(s): contribution per baseline-capacity week at weeks 13-16. Freeze baseline
capacity, contribution weights and all unit accounting rules before assignment.
Contribution subtracts inventory, stockout, labor and operating costs; installation
costs enter NPV rather than being double-counted in this endpoint.
Delta_T = E[Y_i,T(s_F)-Y_i,T(s_T)], equal team weighting, intention to treat.
It is a sequence-policy effect, not an equal-exposure order effect or mediator effect.

SECONDARY ECONOMIC ESTIMANDS
Cumulative transition welfare weeks 1-8, including phase costs.
Total NPV weeks 1-16. Baseline-period real currency; freeze labor/accounting prices.
Annual real discount 5%, weekly factor (1.05)^(-1/52); sensitivities 0% and 10% annual.
Report undiscounted transition contribution separately. Model beta=.95 is illustrative
and not a weekly calibration. Extrapolated continuation values are separately labeled
assumption-based projections, never empirical results.

MEASUREMENTS
Usage and availability separately; recommendations shown, task attempts, scored feedback
actually observed, downtime, defect rates, training, turnover, abandonment, common-task
loss, incentivized reliance/request behavior, expected accuracy and survey trust.
Abandonment means no voluntary use over two consecutive eligible weeks, not no access.
Survey trust is secondary and cannot alone identify the model state.

PRIMARY ANALYSIS
Team-level ANCOVA on the locked endpoint, treatment + block indicators + baseline
endpoint; intention to treat. Report estimate, confidence interval, and block-respecting
randomization test of the sharp null. Use actual assignment probabilities and structure.
Do not treat team-week rows as independent. With few clusters, emphasize randomization
inference and limitations. Confirmatory endpoint tested two-sided at alpha=.05.

MULTIPLICITY AND HETEROGENEITY
Holm across use, behavioral reliance, decision error, survey trust, abandonment.
Economic secondary summaries receive intervals; significance claims across primary,
transition welfare and NPV use Holm. The primary contrast remains explicitly identified
as primary. Continuous baseline-readiness interaction exploratory; no optimal cutpoint
search. Distinguish a null average from absence of heterogeneous effects.

EXPOSURE ACCOUNTING
Log calendar access, cumulative availability, scored opportunities, technical intensity,
training and actual use. Show calendar trajectories and descriptive exposure trajectories.
Do not condition the primary ITT on post-treatment experience. Equal dates, equal exposure
and identical histories cannot all be imposed. Exposure is policy-dependent; regressions
controlling it do not generally identify mediation. No unrestricted causal decomposition
into time, skill and trust is identified by this two-policy design.

OPTIONAL MECHANISM RESCUE
Only after the primary endpoint: independently randomize an extra genuine scored
validation block to ALL teams, stratified by original assignment and baseline readiness.
Separately budget and define an endpoint before rescue assignment. Estimate rescue ITT
and sequence interaction. Do not claim an isolated trust effect: skill, attention and
incentive understanding may also change. If a separate nonuser-only pilot is run, its
selected population is not a common principal stratum across original sequence arms.

THREATS AND IDENTIFICATION
Consistency: versions, intensities, schedules and follow-up defined.
Exchangeability: audited randomized sequence assignment. Positivity within every block.
Interference: no interference beyond the merged assignment cluster; log contamination.
Common time shocks: synchronized phases; arm-specific shocks remain possible.
Selection: no completer-only headline results. Turnover is policy-dependent composition.
Measurement: blind benchmark scoring, immutable denominator, fixed cost weights.
Missing outcomes: distinguish closure, true zero and unrecorded accounts. Closure gets
a predeclared terminal accounting value, never silently zero or deletion. Show missingness
by arm and reason. Report justified bounded-outcome sensitivity intervals; MAR imputation
only under an explicit defensible assumption. Wide bounds mean causal precision is absent.

POWER AND FEASIBILITY
No pilot variance is known. Synthetic stationary outcome variance and AR(1) assumptions
imply 80% MDE .635 at N=96; .492 if baseline R2=.4 is real. At synthetic effect .2214,
approximately 790 teams, or 474 with that adjustment, are required before attrition and
multiplicity. These are normal planning approximations, not a funded cluster count.
Obtain blinded baseline variance, currency-valued minimum worthwhile effect, implementation
capacity, data agreements and site permission before fixing sample size. A small pilot
can assess fidelity/measurement; it cannot establish the modest effect in this illustration.

FALSIFICATION AND INTERPRETATION
An exposure-saturation disappearance is timing/learning evidence, not permanent path
dependence. Persistent final-quality gaps indicate fidelity, not the hypothesized state.
Measure optional feedback requests on common tasks. Test whether genuine validation
changes reliance and outcomes with adequate precision. Reversal supports an experience
pathway but is not proof of trust mediation. Finite persistence never proves permanence.
A null with wide confidence intervals is inconclusive. Rational avoidance of bad advice
is not a failure to be data-driven.

DATA ACCESS
No authorized operational data. MOPS public documentation/tables are candidate descriptive
context, while linked confidential Census microdata require approved access. No application
was made. PITEC is a literature-based descriptive candidate; current access not verified.
Neither dataset supplies the randomized, dense exposure/behavior history required here.

CENTRAL R1: SEPARATE THEORETICAL COMPARISONS
The main article now solves a Bayesian diagnosis/remediation model. The original
field design above identifies its stated rollout-policy ITT, not the new structural
parameters or the screening-versus-diagnosis decomposition. The generic carryover
simulation is a nonstructural inference stress test, not trust validation.
An equal-count theoretical benchmark assigns one shadow diagnostic per ordered
policy, with production on common dates; early and late diagnostic precision can
differ. A compulsory-completion design removes the option to avoid remediation.
A separate adaptive stopping design would have to randomize complete policy rules
that explicitly permit no repair and count all assigned teams, including those
that stop. Restricting analysis to repaired teams would condition on treatment.
Neither variant has been run or recruited.

For rational-mechanism measurement, record elicited expected FINAL performance
before and after observed physical repair, beliefs about persistent effectiveness,
knowledge of repair success, and verified public validation results. Record
validation fees, delays, precision and all disclosures. A known reset or free
revealing validation predicts disappearance of history-based posterior differences
under the stated rational model; installation alone need not. High-type false
negatives conditional on bad history are not evidence that extra information
lowers ex-ante optimized welfare. These are theory-derived hypotheses only.
